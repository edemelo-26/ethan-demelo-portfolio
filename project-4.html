Who the work is for, and why

The course brief identifies USDA as the client and NGA’s Tearline program as the route for commercial imagery. Our team is investigating how geographic conditions, livestock networks, environmental conditions and surveillance could support competing explanations of screwworm transmission pathways from Guatemala through Mexico toward South Texas.

The intended audience is a decision-maker who needs a defensible intelligence estimate: an executive assessment, chronology, geospatial analysis, competing hypotheses, an evidence matrix, justified confidence and explicit information gaps. The brief also calls for methods that later student and graduate research teams can carry forward.

Project purpose and audience: original GEOG 476 “New World Screwworm Transmission Pathways” brief, supplied through the Hub; pages 1–8. The client project and final assessment remain in progress.

My responsibility across the research team

As Ingestor, I am involved across the research streams. My responsibility is to verify the team’s contributed evidence and methods before integration: trace data back to retained sources, reproduce processing, test calculations, reconcile definitions and versions, and identify what a result can support.

I work through source-linked checks, exceptions and review decisions, then return findings and reproducible evidence packages to Brandon, the project lead who integrates the team’s research (the Hub). That work connects individual research contributions to the team’s assessment. A duplicated report, mismatched observation day or incorrect geographic join can change the apparent evidence for an explanation.

I use Codex-assisted Python and documented review to carry out this work. The records distinguish original source collection, my verification and reproduction, subsequent corrections, human review and the exact version delivered.

What verification involves

I check evidence at several levels: a file check confirms exact retained bytes; a source-fidelity check compares a table with its recorded source; a calculation reproduction recomputes the method and compares the result. Together, these checks establish what was received, how it was processed and whether the reported output follows from that processing.

A checked “slot” is a position in a data series and can contain a value or an explicit missing entry. A source “pin” is a recorded file identity used to bind a result to its input version. These counts describe checks, not independent animals, detections or evidence that a transmission hypothesis is true.

What the verification changed

Source review determines how evidence can enter the assessment. My checks preserve missing rainfall instead of manufacturing dry days, separate publication dates from event dates, retain animal/trap and source-measure distinctions, and identify duplicate reports before aggregation. Geographic and product differences travel with the results.

In the rainfall work, reproducing the calculations also exposed a meaningful interpretation issue: an error normalized by the whole month can look smaller than the error in the missing portion being estimated. I retained the failed scores and timing assumptions in the comparison so they could be evaluated rather than hidden by a favorable aggregate.
