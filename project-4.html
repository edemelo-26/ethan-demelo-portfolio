Screwworm Evidence & Data QA

Checking whether contributed GIS and environmental evidence can be reproduced and used together without losing its source meaning.

The problem

Our team is examining geographic, environmental, livestock-system, and surveillance evidence for a New World screwworm capstone. Contributor files can use different units, reporting periods, spatial supports, and quality flags. Those differences need to remain visible when evidence moves into a shared analysis.

A rainfall pilot made that problem concrete: a missing daily reading, a questioned observation, and an estimated value need different treatment. Replacing a blank with zero or silently relabeling an estimate would change what the record means.

My role

I work as the team’s Ingestor, using an AI-assisted workflow to inspect contributor packages, check provenance and compatibility, reproduce supplied processing, and document limitations for review. Teammates contribute the source analyses; my contribution is their reproducible examination and qualification.

For my Draft 2 report, I reran a retained radar workflow and checked its calculations using separate routines with Codex assistance. Other intake work includes checking table arithmetic and confirming that rebuilt outputs match the supplied release.

Data and method

My bounded example was a station-level rainfall pilot for Hondo, Texas, focused on 14 missing days in June 2026.

The rainfall example used contributor-supplied station records from NOAA GHCN-Daily and retained MRMS RadarOnly_QPE_24H precipitation estimates. I retained the supplied native grid definition and checked units, quality flags, date alignment, and the difference between station and raster-cell measurements.

Table intake and rebuild checks

A separate, completed intake of a teammate’s six-report derivative checked 904 supplied values, independently recomputed 412 arithmetic controls, and matched seven rebuilt outputs byte for byte. These are technical verification counts from the saved intake record, rather than counts of disease cases or a scientific validation result.

What I found

The rainfall outputs were reproducible within the retained test package, but the tested radar estimates exceeded the pilot’s gap-error criterion. I kept the comparison flagged and documented its limits for review. Reproducing a table establishes computational agreement within the tested scope; accuracy and suitability for a new analysis require further evidence.

Reporting-day alignment, cohort coverage, and the treatment of missing and questioned readings remain material limits. A station-level rainfall check also needs a separate geographic design before it can support broader environmental comparisons.

Next steps

I would confirm the station accumulation intervals, expand matched validation under a documented sampling plan, and test a defined research endpoint before integrating a rainfall reconstruction. The handoff needs to retain estimate flags, contributor credit, unresolved dependencies, and the exact review status.
